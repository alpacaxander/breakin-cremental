# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## What this is

BreakIn cremental: a horizontal breakout incremental game in Godot 4.7 (GDScript). Before each timed round you place items on a hand-made level, then play it to earn coins; coins buy nodes in an upgrade web. The pitch, core loop, demo scope, open questions and risks are in `docs/design.md`.

**Current state: playable, with effects, tuned by the bot and not yet by hand.** The repo was purged on 2026-11-12 (commit `1aa951b`) and the demo described in `docs/design.md` is being rebuilt from the docs, in the steps of `docs/architecture.md` ("Build order"). All six steps are done: the last was tuning the numbers with the balance bot. Two audit rounds against the docs followed, one before the tuning and a shorter one after it. What exists: the project skeleton, the render chain with bloom and the CRT (`src/main.*`), navigation and the palette; the rules (`src/sim/`: a `Round` that runs headless, with swept collision) and the twelve kinds (`src/kinds/`, `data/kinds/`); the save, the settings and the 31 upgrades (`src/autoload/progress.gd`, `src/autoload/settings.gd`, `data/upgrades/`); the three levels and their reader (`levels/`, `src/content/levels.gd`); the synthesised cues, the placeholder music and `Sfx` (`assets/audio/`, `src/autoload/sfx.gd`); the theme and shared widgets (`src/ui/`); and every screen: the main menu and Settings (`src/menu/`), the upgrade screen (`src/hub/`) and the level screen with Setup, Play and the Summary (`src/level/`). The game can be played from the menu through a level and back, and `tests/cases/loop.gd` walks that whole loop with input events. There is a balance bot (`tests/balance.gd`). The effects (`src/level/fx/fx.gd`) decorate a round: squares, shockwaves, light pools, the trail, pop-ups, shake, freezes, and bloom that climbs with the combo. The numbers under `data/`, the levels' targets, the brick HP of levels 1 and 3 and the effects' `COMBO_TOP` are tuned by the bot and not yet by hand. The web has 70 nodes, one per purchase (31 upgrades, each rank a copy of its upgrade on a node of its own, the copies sharing one price ladder, laid out as a pinwheel round the seed), 103,044 coins and 3 keys in four categories (Arena, Placeables, Bricks, Abilities) and three price bands, one per level (`docs/design/upgrades.md`); Splitter Splits has been replaced by Splitter Respawn (a broken Splitter comes back after 6, 4 or 2 s; a Splitter now always makes exactly one new ball, out of its far side). The newest work is the web's redesign (one node per purchase, keys, previews, undo; see below). Before it came the ability system: a Power Brick (a special brick) that breaks makes a drop, which falls toward the paddle side and, if the paddle catches it, becomes a charge, a ball in reserve that goes onto the paddle at once when none waits there; a click or Space fires it carrying the selected ability (Zap or Blast, cycled with the wheel or Q/E), which goes off at the first level brick it hits, and then it plays on as a plain ball. A held charge, a falling drop or a ball of the opening volley still to come keeps the round alive. Multiball (more balls behind the first launch, 0.15 s apart, on one line) replaces Spare Ball; the explosive brick, the Ball Brick and the brick-mod kind are gone, so nothing chains and Zap is an ability rather than a placed mark. There is a test level for it, `levels/level_0.tscn` ("Abilities Test": a column of painted Power Bricks in front of a block in HP bands), which is not counted or shown in the upgrade screen and is opened from the Admin panel (` in a debug build). The bot catches drops and fires charges (`charges=hold` or `launch`, `ability=zap`, `blast` or `first`), and the retune after the ability system set Combo to 4, the Booster to 5, Paddle Size to 20 ×4, Power Brick to 25 ×4 and Zap and Blast to 60, and the targets of levels 2 and 3 to 384 and 2048; since then each level's first clear pays a key, Zap, Blast and Overgrowth cost a key each and no coins, and a bought node can be undone for a refund (`Progress.keys`, `buy`, `undo`; the save, now version 2, records the nodes bought and refunds a version 1 file's ranks as coins). The hub shows the keys (a counter beside the coins, key prices on Zap, Blast and Overgrowth, and a line in the level panel) and previews, dimmed nodes it can't buy yet next to the open ones and behind an open key node, and offers undo: a right click on an owned node, or Undo in the detail panel, while every other owned node still reaches the seed without it. Before it, the newest were Spare Ball and the Ball Brick, both now removed (balls in reserve: one per rank at the start of a round and one per Ball Brick broken; with every rank they cut level 3's lost-ball rounds from 85–90% to 10–35% and multiplied what it paid), the Guide (a line, while the ball waits on the paddle, along the way it would launch; ranks lengthen it, and Guide Bounces lets it bounce; the launch aim sways rather than being random, wider once the Guide is owned), the Tough Brick (3 HP), Growth (an area laid over the bricks that adds 1 HP to each level brick in it, once at the start of a round and again 0.5 s apart for each rank of Growth Pulses, which was Pulse Rate and no longer speeds Refresh; it changed after every bot measurement), Refresh (a placed block that, when hit, brings back the nearest broken level bricks; taken out of the web, so it is in the code but can't be had in game, like the Bumper) and Overgrowth (every broken level brick comes back 8 s later). Every hit on a level brick scores 1 point, and a level is cleared by reaching its target score (`target` in each level scene: 28, 384, 2048), not by breaking every brick. The effects' strengths and timings are first values that have been checked in still frames and never felt in motion. Each level should go through three stages (almost unbeatable, beatable with skill, easy), each longer on later levels; the goals are in `docs/design.md` ("Progression"), and the bot's campaign prints where each stage ends against them. Its better players aim at cells a level marks with the aim tile (level 3's tunnel mouth). The bot is the weakest stand-in on level 3 and for the Booster, Wall Segment and Shockwave. A hit that leaves a level brick standing grows the combo as a break does. The Booster is a gate (16 × 96, rotatable) that the ball passes through and leaves faster, no longer a round item it bounces off. A Booster adds 100 units/s (20% of the ball's 500) and boosts cap at 1000; level 2 is a 4×4 checkerboard of big squares. With the layout search, on average over 20 seeds (`bot campaign bases=20`; 10 for the other skills), the average player first clears level 1 on round 5.0, level 2 on 13.1 and level 3 on 23.6 (range 15 to 35; the good player 3.9, 13.5 and 18.9, the clumsy one 8.4, 20.6 and 33.5), against the demo's goal of about 30; in one probed average campaign (base 0) its level 1 stages end on rounds 3 and 7 (goals 2 and 6), level 2's on 1 and 21 (4 and 10), level 3's on 5 and 21 (6 and 16), and over four seeds a stage end moves by up to 12 rounds. Level 2's stage 1 comes early and its stage 2 late. Level 3 is still a lottery: its rounds pay from nothing to thousands, though the ball is seldom lost early any more (3 rounds in 52), and a round there on arrival pays more than one on level 2 in three of four campaigns. Before level 3's first clear 33 to 37 of the 70 nodes are bought on average, so most of level 3's price band goes unbought. Zap and Blast pay about the same; with every rank Zap pays more on level 3. With every rank level 3's best combos pass 300, past the effects' `COMBO_TOP` of 160. Level 3's wall is columns 32–63 (8 empty squares of 4 × 2 cells in front, 2 behind; 256 bricks, 326 hits). Sealing the paddle side with Wall Segments and blocks paid less than playing, before the ability system. Campaigns buy Overgrowth only with the key from level 3's first clear; with every upgrade it makes full wipes on levels 1 and 2 rare. Nobody has played it by hand or listened to the audio.

## Commands

Godot, xvfb-run and Node are not on PATH. They come from the Nix dev shell. `tests/run.sh` enters it by itself; for anything else run `nix develop`, or prefix the command with `nix develop -c`.

```sh
tests/run.sh import           # import assets: once in a new checkout, and after adding files
tests/run.sh test             # headless self-check: "ok" or FAIL lines, exit 1 on a failure
tests/run.sh test rules       # only tests/cases/rules.gd
tests/run.sh boot             # the main scene, headless, for 5 seconds; fails on any error
tests/run.sh shots            # screenshots of the real game, bloom and CRT included, to tests/out/shots/
tests/run.sh shots hub fx     # only those shot lists
tests/run.sh bot              # the balance bot's report: every level fresh and maxed, then a campaign (minutes)
tests/run.sh bot check        # the bot's own self-check, 2 seconds
tests/run.sh bot batch level=1 ranks=fresh skill=all seeds=20 layout=empty   # one table; keys are in tests/balance.gd
tests/run.sh bot campaign skill=average rounds=400
tests/run.sh bot campaign skill=average bases=20   # 20 campaigns (base 0, 100 ...) in a row and the spread of their first clears (about an hour)
nix develop -c godot          # open the editor
```

- `tests/run.sh` moves `user://` to `tests/out/user`, so worktrees running at once share nothing and no run touches the player's save. `tests/run.sh <godot args>` passes anything else to Godot.
- Tests are plain checks with no framework: one case file per lane in `tests/cases/` and one shot list per lane in `tests/shots/`, plus the few that belong to no lane (the fuzz and play-test cases; see "Tests" in `docs/architecture.md`). `tests/test.gd` and `tests/shots.gd` describe the format. A Godot error during a case fails it.
- Tests and shots run as scenes, because a `godot -s` script cannot see the autoloads. Screenshots need the virtual display; `--headless` renders nothing.
- There is no lint or build step.

## Docs

Every value in the docs is provisional. Where the docs give no number, pick a sensible starting value and keep it easy to tune.

- `docs/design.md`: the summary. Pitch, loop, mechanics, controls, the demo's scope and the open questions (coin formula, combo numbers, arena size ceiling, screens with no mockup).
- `docs/design/overview.md`: screen flow (menu → play scene switching between Upgrades and Level), level phases (setup → 20 s play → summary, with Retry) and end conditions, how coins are paid, save contents.
- `docs/design/levels.md`: grid rules, per-level arena size and zoom, and the three demo layouts.
- `docs/design/physics.md`: ball and paddle behaviour (constant speed, paddle bounce angle, minimum angles, launch, collision).
- `docs/design/placeables.md`: the three kinds of placeables, their counts and saved layouts.
- `docs/design/upgrades.md`: the upgrade web, its four categories (Arena, Placeables, Bricks, Abilities) and the upgrade screen, with keys, previews and undo. It draws the demo's 70-node web and lists its 31 upgrades with effects and costs, what each special brick does and what the abilities do.
- `docs/art.md`: every size, position, effect, font and UI rule, in pixels at a 1280 × 720 frame. It links the mockup artifact; if they disagree, fix whichever is wrong so they match. It lists where the mockups are currently behind the docs.
- `docs/palette.md`: the only place colours are defined, each hue tied to one meaning.
- `docs/audio.md`: how sound is synthesised by a script (reused from the purged build), the cue list, playback rules and the placeholder music loop.
- `docs/architecture.md`: how the code is organised. Rules (`src/sim/`, no nodes) are kept apart from screens and data; it gives the scene tree, autoloads, the round's signals, the file layout, the tests and the build order. Follow it when writing code.

## Design constraints that shape the code

These are spread across several docs:

- **Arena:** the paddle is on the left edge and moves up and down; the back wall is on the right and the paddle side is open. Losing the last ball ends the round, unless a charge is held, a drop is still falling or the opening volley has a ball to come; the bricks broken so far are still paid.
- **Grid:** each level sets its row count, and columns = rows × 4. The base is 64 × 16 with 16 × 32 px cells; other sizes zoom everything inside the arena by 16 ÷ rows, while the arena frame and the UI stay fixed. A brick is 1 column × 2 rows (a 16 × 64 cell, drawn at 14 × 62) with HP (default 1) shown by its colour.
- **Ball:** constant speed. Bounces change its direction only; speed changes only through boosts (Booster, Shockwave, Rear Paddle), which last until the ball next touches the paddle, except a Booster's, which lasts the round. Collision is swept, and a brick hit resolves against one edge. The paddle chases the mouse at paddle speed.
- **Placeables:** free-form items go anywhere clear of bricks and the paddle lane (some rotate), placeable bricks snap to empty grid cells, and areas snap to any cell and are not solid. Each copy of an unlock upgrade owned (its rank) allows one more; special bricks follow the same rule and are picked again every attempt.
- **Coins:** calculated from the round's tally of hits, bricks broken (by type) and combos, not handed out per brick. Every hit on a level brick scores 1 point, broken or not (a break scores the kind's worth), and every one grows the combo as a break does; a running combo multiplies every point. Placed bricks pay nothing, don't count toward a clear and grow the combo only when they break (Growth and Refresh blocks never do).
- **Hand-made content:** each level is its own scene, painted in the editor (for example a TileMapLayer) with per-brick HP. The upgrade web is a scene that holds layout and connections; each upgrade's cost, growth, max rank and effect are in its own resource file. There is no procedural generation.
- **Structure:** rules, screens and data are separate layers (see `docs/architecture.md`). Adding a level, an upgrade, a placeable or a brick kind should mean adding a file.
- **Save:** one slot holding the coin balance, each upgrade's rank, the web nodes bought, the highest level unlocked and each level's placed layout; keys are not stored but counted from the levels cleared less the key nodes owned. Nothing else about a level in progress is saved. Settings are stored separately.
- **Rendering:** stretch mode `canvas_items`, aspect `keep`, at 1280 × 720. Shapes are flat and drawn in the engine (`ColorRect`, `Polygon2D`, `draw_rect`), with no textures. Use squares, never circles; the Booster placeable is the one exception. Glow comes only from WorldEnvironment bloom with HDR 2D on: the threshold sits above the slate neutrals and lit colours are pushed over it. White is reserved for the ball and its trail.

## History

The purged implementation at `1aa951b^` was a different design (a "Peglin, but breakout" roguelike run), with `src/`, a headless `tests/test.gd` self-check and generated art and audio. Use `git show 1aa951b^:<path>` to reuse a piece, such as ball and paddle physics, but don't restore it wholesale.
